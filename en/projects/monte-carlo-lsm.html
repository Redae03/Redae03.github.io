American options with Monte Carlo and Longstaff-Schwartz

Stochastic calculus project · ENSA Agadir · supervised by Prof. B. El Asri · 2026

American put priced at 4.46 against 4.478 in the reference paper; estimator variance cut 6.8-fold with a control variate.

Simulator (French interface) Code (GitHub) Report (PDF, French)

Question

An American option can be exercised at any time: its price has no closed form and depends on an unknown optimal exercise rule. The goal was to build a reliable simulation engine, measure its accuracy and reduce its computational cost.

Method

  1. Random variate generation (inversion, Box-Muller) and geometric Brownian motion paths, Euler-Maruyama scheme.
  2. European options by Monte Carlo with confidence intervals and 1/√N convergence.
  3. Variance reduction: antithetic variates (variance computed on pair averages) and the control variate e^(−rT)S_T − S₀.
  4. Longstaff-Schwartz: regression of continuation values on a polynomial basis over in-the-money paths, 50 exercise dates, 100,000 paths.
  5. Validation against Longstaff and Schwartz (2001) and a Bermudan binomial tree; PyQt5 application.

Results

On an at-the-money European call, estimator variance falls 2.0-fold with antithetic variates and 6.8-fold with the control variate, for the same number of simulations. Reviewing the code, I fixed two bugs: the antithetic variance was computed as if draws were independent (reported gain of 1.0 instead of 2.0), and the control-variate method was rejected by the summary report.

Limitations and next steps

  • The LSM estimator is biased downward because the estimated exercise rule is sub-optimal. An upper bound (Andersen-Broadie dual method) would bracket the price.
  • The regression basis and the number of exercise dates are chosen empirically.
