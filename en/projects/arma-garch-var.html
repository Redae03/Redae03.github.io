Volatility and Value-at-Risk of Managem with ARMA–GARCH

Time-series project · ENSA Agadir · 2026

306 months of prices: persistent volatility (half-life about 6 months), a significant leverage effect, and an information leak detected in the neural-network benchmark.

Simulator (French interface) Code (GitHub) Report (PDF, French)

Question

Model the dynamics and risk of a mining stock listed in Casablanca, sensitive to metal prices, then compare an econometric approach with a machine-learning method for forecasting.

Method

  1. ADF and Phillips-Perron unit-root tests on the price, its log and returns.
  2. ARMA(1,1) for the mean, residual diagnostics (Ljung-Box, Jarque-Bera, ARCH-LM).
  3. GARCH(1,1), TARCH and EGARCH for volatility; one-month conditional VaR.
  4. Out-of-sample forecasting (80/20 split) against an MLP neural network; regression of the price on gold, silver and copper (2022–2026).
  5. Estimation in EViews, then replication in Python (statsmodels, arch, scikit-learn).

Results

ItemResult
ADF price / returns5.88 (p = 1.00) / −15.17 (p < 0.0001)
GARCH(1,1): α + β0.89, half-life about 6 months
EGARCH: asymmetry−0.133 (p = 0.0001), leverage effect
1-month 95% VaR (GARCH)16.0% potential loss
Out-of-sample RMSEARMA 0.089 · zero forecast 0.092 · MLP 0.098

The original report showed a large advantage for the neural network (RMSE 0.052). Replicating it, this gap only appears if the return being forecast is mistakenly included among the inputs: the advantage appears to come from an information leak. Correctly specified, no model clearly beats a zero forecast, consistent with hard-to-predict monthly returns.

Limitations and next steps

  • The normal VaR likely underestimates tail risk given fat-tailed residuals. A Student-t distribution and a Kupiec backtest are the next steps.
  • The gold regression is estimated in levels: a cointegration test would rule out a spurious correlation.
